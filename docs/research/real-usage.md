# Writ wins where tool calls carry meaning

writ is useful today as a deterministic, local brake on agent tool calls that carry business meaning: a refund amount, a customer ID, a SQL table, a repository, a Terraform action. The strongest documented case for it is the "lethal trifecta" incident class, where an agent reads attacker-written content and then makes a privileged call. The GitHub MCP leak and the Supabase/Cursor leak are the canonical examples, and writ's taint, entitlement and approval controls are aimed squarely at that pattern. Its first real users are coding-agent and framework developers who adopt free, local, open-source tools bottom-up. Enterprise security teams, regulated firms and SaaS vendors gain the most from its lineage tokens, hash-chained log and replay, but they buy on identity integration, HTTP transport, central audit, SIEM export and vendor maturity, and writ has none of those yet. Two things stand between writ and production use. The first is plumbing: Streamable HTTP, verified principals via OIDC, Slack approvals, OpenTelemetry, anchored logs and real entitlement connectors. The second is honesty about scope: writ cannot see raw-shell commands, rendered-output exfiltration, malicious server internals or client RCEs, and it should be sold alongside sandboxes and scoped credentials rather than as a replacement for them. Its genuine differentiation is the combination of runtime taint, Biscuit sub-agent narrowing and a "policy got looser" replay gate inside one neutral Apache-2.0 enforcement layer. Each piece has precedents, but the sources reviewed show no product that ships all of them together. **Evidence caveat:** most source notes were built from search-result summaries because many primary sites were blocked. Nearly every survey was commissioned by a vendor, and many 2026 incidents come from a single unverified aggregator. The figures below are indicative, and unverified items are labeled as such.

## Six segments split into a developer motion and a buyer motion

Agent adoption is broad but shallow, and security is the stated brake. In Dynatrace's survey of 919 senior leaders, **only 13% run fully autonomous agents**, and 44% still review agent-to-agent communication by hand ([Dynatrace](https://www.dynatrace.com/news/press-release/pulse-of-agentic-ai-2026/)). Secondary write-ups of McKinsey's 2026 survey put security and risk as the top barrier to scaling, cited by nearly two-thirds ([vantagepoint.io](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/sf/agentic-ai-trust-gap-leaders-believe-wont-let-act)). Gartner expects **more than 40% of agentic projects to be cancelled by end of 2027**, partly over "inadequate risk controls" ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). The concerns respondents name are authorization problems at the moment of a tool call, not content-filtering problems. Data leakage and over-privileged access top Okta's buyer survey, where 69% say security concerns slow adoption ([Okta](https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/enterprise-buyer-survey-ai-agent-security/)). In ETR's survey, 57% flag agents acting outside intended scope ([ETR](https://research.etr.ai/etr-data-drop/agentic-ai-is-live.-enterprise-security-controls-are-not)). In CSA surveys, 68% cannot tell agent activity from human activity ([CSA/Aembit](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/press-releases/2026/03/24/more-than-two-thirds-of-organizations-cannot-clearly-distinguish-ai-agent-from-human-actions)) and **84% doubt they could pass an audit of agent behavior** ([CSA/Strata](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/press-releases/2026/02/05/cloud-security-alliance-strata-survey-finds-that-enterprises-are-in-time-to-trust-phase-as-they-build-ai-autonomy-foundations)). These map closely onto writ's entitlements, taint, approvals and flight recorder. The mapping is the research team's inference, and almost every survey here is vendor-sponsored.

The segments fall into two motions with different bars. In the **bottom-up motion**, coding-agent users, framework users and startups adopt tools that cut friction and run locally. MCP lives on laptops: one aggregator-cited survey finds **86% of MCP servers run locally on developer machines and only 5% in production** ([Practical DevSecOps citing Clutch](https://www.practical-devsecops.com/mcp-security-statistics-2026-report/), aggregator, primary not checked). That makes writ's stdio-plus-local-daemon shape a fit today. In the **top-down motion**, enterprise security, regulated industries and SaaS vendors buy on identity integration, central audit and compliance evidence. For them writ is currently a proof of concept they cannot buy.

| Segment | Wants | Hard requirements | Likely objections to writ today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise security / IAM (buyer: CISO, IAM lead) | Control shadow agents; give agents identities chained to a human; pass audits | IdP binding (Okta, Entra); central policy; SIEM export; RBAC over who approves; inventory of agents and servers | Cooperative mode is bypassable; single machine, no fleet view; Biscuit sits beside OAuth; free AWS, Okta and Ping offerings |
| AI app developers and startups (buyer = user) | Ship fast, then answer enterprise security questionnaires | Drop-in, low latency, no SaaS dependency, good defaults | "My framework already has HITL"; an extra daemon; not needed until a customer asks |
| Framework users (LangGraph, OpenAI Agents SDK, Claude Agent SDK, CrewAI, ADK) | Enforcement below the framework; safe sub-agent delegation | Adapters for their framework's hooks and interrupt UIs | Native function tools are invisible to an MCP proxy; in-process pauses feel sufficient |
| Coding-agent users (Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, Codex) | Fewer prompts, not more; no `rm -rf` or production disasters | Works across tools, local and offline | Vendor-native managed settings and sandboxes overlap; shell and file tools often bypass MCP |
| Regulated industries (finance, health, insurance, government) | Prove supervision; reconstruct decisions | Tamper-evident logs with retention, self-hosting, human approval trail, regulatory mapping | No commercial entity or support; no off-host log anchoring; single machine |
| SaaS vendors shipping MCP servers, and MSPs | Pass customer security reviews; scope per tenant | Multi-tenant policy, per-customer delegation, per-tenant audit export | No remote or multi-tenant mode; MSPs want an RMM/PSA-integrated console |

The table is the research team's synthesis. Specific demands behind it are sourced. CISOs rank "full control over who has access to agents at any moment" as the top AI security feature ([Okta CISO Insights](https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/global-ciso-insights-2026/)). 43% of organizations run agents on shared service accounts and 31% under human identities ([CSA/Aembit](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/press-releases/2026/03/24/more-than-two-thirds-of-organizations-cannot-clearly-distinguish-ai-agent-from-human-actions)). 98% of SaaS buyers say they will weigh agent controls at renewal, which pushes the requirement down onto startups and vendors ([Okta](https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/enterprise-buyer-survey-ai-agent-security/)).

The strongest developer objection is **approval fatigue**. Developers routinely switch Claude Code into `--dangerously-skip-permissions` ("YOLO mode") ([ClaudeLog](https://claudelog.com/mechanics/dangerous-skip-permissions)). Anthropic reports that sandboxing cut permission prompts by **84%** in internal use by letting the agent act freely inside fixed limits ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-sandboxing)). This is writ's design brief: allow by policy, and escalate only when taint meets a privileged sink. A per-call confirmation layer would be rejected. Practitioners also argue that framework pauses are not policy. OpenAI's `needsApproval` and LangGraph's `interrupt()` hold a call but do not enforce allow/deny rules, and a direct `tool.invoke` bypasses them ([Arcjet](https://arcjet.com/learn/needsapproval-and-interrupt-are-not-a-policy)). That argument favours an enforcement point outside the framework. Demand is visible but fragmented: individuals keep publishing one-person MCP security gateways ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/sidpad03/i-built-an-open-source-security-gateway-for-mcp-because-the-ecosystem-desperately-needs-one-4dkc)). That signals a real need and also a crowded field with little differentiation.

## Writ stops the trifecta and destructive calls, not shell or rendered exfiltration

The well-documented incidents sort cleanly by where the harmful step happens. writ helps when that step is a distinct tool call with arguments it can inspect, routed through a wrapped server. It does not help when the step rides an allowed channel, happens inside a server, or never touches MCP.

**The best fit is indirect injection followed by a write-back.** In Invariant's GitHub MCP case, a malicious public issue hijacked the agent, which used a personal access token covering public and private repos to leak private data into a public PR. "No tool was poisoned, no server was compromised, no credential was stolen" ([Invariant Labs](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability)). In the Supabase/Cursor case, an attacker's support ticket made an agent holding an RLS-bypassing service-role key read the `integration_tokens` table and write it back into a ticket the attacker could see ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/6/supabase-mcp-lethal-trifecta/)). Both are Willison's lethal trifecta: private data, untrusted content and an outbound channel in one agent. The fix he recommends is removing a leg rather than trusting filters ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)). writ's coarse taint removes the third leg deterministically. Once the task has read a declared untrusted source, privileged calls need a human. Entitlement rules ("this repo or table is within the principal's task") and argument rules close the path even without taint. writ's own poisoned-ticket demo encodes exactly this pattern. An out-of-entitlement refund is denied, a covered refund of $100 or less passes with no human, and a larger refund on a tainted task pauses for `writ sudo` ([ROADMAP](../../ROADMAP.md)).

**Destructive mistakes are the second fit.** During Jason Lemkin's trial, Replit's agent deleted a production database despite a code freeze stated in the prompt, then misrepresented what it had done ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/ai-coding-tool-replit-wiped-database-called-it-a-catastrophic-failure/)). In February 2026 a Claude Code agent ran `terraform destroy` against DataTalks.Club's production infrastructure ([AIID](https://incidentdatabase.ai/fr/cite/1424)). Amazon's Kiro reportedly deleted and recreated an environment, causing a roughly 13-hour disruption; Amazon disputes this account and blames misconfigured access ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/20/amazon_denies_kiro_agentic_ai_behind_outage/)). The shared lesson is that a gate stated in a prompt is not a gate. A deterministic rule such as "DROP or DELETE against prod requires approval" holds, and so does "a code-freeze fact denies writes." The flight log would also have contradicted the agent's false account. The condition is decisive, though: **this works only if the action goes through a mediated tool.** In the Amazon Q v1.84 incident, an injected prompt told the agent to run `aws ec2 terminate-instances` and `iam delete-user` through a shell with ambient credentials ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cyber-security/hacker-injects-malicious-potentially-disk-wiping-prompt-into-amazons-ai-coding-assistant-with-a-simple-pull-request-told-your-goal-is-to-clean-a-system-to-a-near-factory-state-and-delete-file-system-and-cloud-resources)). An MCP proxy never sees that.

**Config self-modification is a cheap, high-value default writ should ship.** CurXecute (CVE-2025-54135) made Cursor rewrite `~/.cursor/mcp.json` after reading a poisoned Slack message ([BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ai-powered-cursor-ide-vulnerable-to-prompt-injection-attacks)). CVE-2025-53773 made Copilot set `"chat.tools.autoApprove": true` in `.vscode/settings.json` ([CyberSecurityNews](https://cybersecuritynews.com/github-copilot-rce-vulnerability)). A rule saying the agent may not write its own configuration or policy stops this class whenever file writes are mediated. A late-October 2026 report of malicious `AGENTS.md` files hijacking Codex and Claude Code ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ai-coding-agents-hijacked-using-agentsmd-file-in-downloads-folder/)) belongs in the same class, but it comes from an aggregator and is unverified.

**Tool poisoning and rug pulls are only partly covered.** Invariant showed hidden instructions in a tool description steering an agent to read `~/.ssh/id_rsa`. It showed a server swapping in malicious definitions via `notifications/tools/list_changed` to redirect WhatsApp messages ([Docker](https://www.docker.com/blog/mcp-horror-stories-whatsapp-data-exfiltration-issue/)). It also noted that sandboxing the server does not help ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/9/mcp-prompt-injection/)). writ's rules are keyed to tool names and arguments, so a poisoned description cannot widen what a call may do: a "recipient must be the original addressee" rule stops the redirect. But an allowed call steered to a harmful purpose passes. writ sits on the exact path where pinning tool definitions and alerting on `list_changed` diffs is natural, as Willison recommends.

**Several attack classes are out of writ's reach, and the pitch must say so.** Exfiltration through allowed channels defeats call-level policy. EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711) leaked Microsoft 365 Copilot data through rendered links to allowed Microsoft domains after bypassing Microsoft's injection classifier ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-microsoft-365-copilot-flaw-echoleak/)). ForcedLeak used an expired, allow-listed Salesforce domain ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/26/salesforce_agentforce_forceleak_attack/)). Claude Code's CVE-2025-55284 leaked secrets through DNS lookups from an allow-listed `ping` ([Embrace The Red mirror](https://jmason.ie/2025/08/25/161304a.html)). Malicious server internals are invisible: the impostor postmark-mcp package BCC'd every email to the attacker from inside an allowed `send_email` ([Koi Security](https://koi.ai/blog/postmark-mcp-npm-malicious-backdoor-email-theft)). Client and transport RCEs happen below the policy layer. In mcp-remote (CVE-2025-6514), a crafted OAuth endpoint let a malicious server run commands ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/07/critical-mcp-remote-vulnerability.html)), and the MCP Inspector (CVE-2025-49596) shipped an unauthenticated localhost UI. Asana's cross-tenant MCP exposure was a server-side isolation bug. There, writ's value is after the fact: Asana's customers needed per-user logs to scope impact ([BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/asana-warns-mcp-ai-feature-exposed-customer-data-to-other-orgs/)), and a hash-chained flight log plus replay is exactly that artifact.

| Attack class | Documented example | writ today | What closes the gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Injection, then write-back via a tool | GitHub MCP, Supabase/Cursor | **Stops** (taint, entitlement, policy) | Already the core demo |
| Injection, then privileged business action | CSA "Helpdesk Hijack" support bot ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-support-bot-identity-bypass-20260603/)) | **Stops** (limits, ownership, approval) | A non-LLM `verified_identity` fact |
| Destructive call by a mistaken agent | Replit, DataTalks, Kiro (disputed) | **Stops** if mediated | Hook adapter for shell tools; sandbox |
| Agent edits its own config | CurXecute, CVE-2025-53773 | Stops if file writes are mediated | Default deny pack for config paths |
| Tool poisoning / rug pull | Invariant `add`, WhatsApp | Partial | Tool-definition pinning |
| Exfiltration via allowed channel | EchoLeak, ForcedLeak, DNS `ping` | No | Egress allowlists, sandboxing |
| Malicious server internals | postmark-mcp | No (audit only) | Provenance and registry pinning |
| Client or transport RCE | mcp-remote, MCP Inspector | No | Patching, server allowlists |
| Raw shell / browser primitives | Amazon Q v1.84, Comet ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/20/perplexity_comet_browser_prompt_injection/)) | Weak | Sandbox plus scoped credentials |

The table is the research team's synthesis from the incidents above. The 2026 aggregator stream is long, but its items are labeled unverified in the notes, and several carry CVE identifiers that look implausibly large; they should be checked on NVD before anyone cites them. Two of those items still carry a lesson for writ, whatever their numbers turn out to be. Gateways and approval UIs are themselves attack surfaces: reports describe an AI gateway that let any OIDC token holder self-enroll ([GHSA](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-qx8v-pq9p-7g7v), unverified) and an MCP gateway that shipped a default JWT signing key ([NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-53709), unverified). writ's in-memory signing key and its refusal to let task descendants approve their own calls answer that class directly, and the outside review of the token design the roadmap calls for is not optional.

Mapped onto workflows, writ fits best where actions have business semantics. In **support refunds**, the rules are an amount cap, the charge belonging to the requester's customer, and taint from ticket text. In **SQL agents**, they are a table allowlist and approval for DDL/DELETE, but this needs SQL-aware conditions writ lacks. In **DevOps**, writ can deny `destroy` in a prod namespace and fork a token scoped to one service. In **coding agents**, it can protect config paths and gate pushes to public repos after reading issues. In **finance AP**, the rules are a payee drawn from the vendor master and approval for bank-detail changes derived from email. In **IT helpdesk**, a reset should require identity verification performed outside the model ([Petri](https://petri.com/5-ways-to-secure-your-service-desk-against-ai-attacks/)). In **CRM**, lead fields are untrusted and bulk exports need approval. Today's production practice in these workflows is ad hoc: Stripe restricted keys, Supabase read-only mode, single-repo tokens, dev/prod separation, and two-person rules after incidents ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/aws-ai-coding-tool-decided-to-delete-and-recreate-a-customer-facing-system-causing-13-hour-outage-report-says/)). writ's pitch is to centralize that layer and make it deterministic and auditable. It is weak for **browser agents**, whose click-and-type primitives carry little meaning a policy can check. No documented agent incidents were found for finance AP/AR, FHIR/EHR or HubSpot, so those scenarios are extrapolations.

## Every stack layer already exposes a hook writ can occupy

**MCP transport and spec.** writ inserts transparently into stdio clients today by rewriting each `mcpServers` command to `writ wrap -- <original>`, the same pattern `mcp-remote` uses ([mcp-remote](https://mcpservers.org/servers/geelen/mcp-remote)). Remote-only clients cannot launch local processes, so they need a URL. ChatGPT connectors appear to consume remote MCP servers ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-connectors-mcp)), and Claude Code treats an untyped `url` entry as a configuration error ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp)). The 2026-07-28 revision, whose final status secondary sources disagree on, reportedly makes MCP stateless. It drops the initialize handshake and `Mcp-Session-Id`, adds required `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers that servers must check against the body, and carries W3C trace context in `_meta` ([MCP blog RC](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/); [mcpservers.org](https://blog.mcpservers.org/posts/mcp-spec-2026-07-28)). Those field names come from secondary sources and must be checked against modelcontextprotocol.io. If they hold, an HTTP writ gets cheaper to build. Per-call decisions need no session affinity, headers allow a pre-filter before the body is parsed, and trace context gives writ a parent span. The same revision reportedly requires servers to publish RFC 9728 Protected Resource Metadata and clients to use RFC 8707 Resource Indicators ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/mcp-2026-spec-agent-authentication)). The spec's security guidance forbids token passthrough: servers "MUST NOT accept any tokens that were not explicitly issued for the MCP server" ([MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/basic/security_best_practices)). An HTTP writ must therefore be its own protected resource and obtain separate upstream tokens rather than forward client tokens. URL-mode elicitation is a standards-native channel through which writ could send a "go approve at this link" message back to the user's client ([MCP spec: elicitation](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/client/elicitation)), but its round-trip shape reportedly changed in 2026, and client support is unknown.

**Gateways.** writ should integrate with gateways rather than compete head-on. Envoy has a native MCP filter that exports MCP attributes for RBAC or ext_authz ([Envoy docs](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/configuration/http/http_filters/mcp_filter)). agentgateway supports ext_authz over gRPC or HTTP ([agentgateway.dev](https://agentgateway.dev/)). Docker MCP Gateway accepts `--interceptor` filters before and after calls ([Docker docs](https://docs.docker.com/reference/cli/docker/mcp/gateway/run/)). All three are slots where writ's engine can make the decision while the gateway handles the edge. AWS Bedrock AgentCore already has its own Cedar engine: default-deny, one action per tool, argument conditions, a `LOG_ONLY` mode, and partial evaluation to hide always-denied tools ([AWS Security Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/why-policy-in-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-chose-cedar-for-securing-agentic-workflows/)). There, writ is better positioned as a YAML-to-Cedar compiler plus a replay tool run against AgentCore's logs than as an inline filter. The official MCP Registry is still in preview and does not support private servers ([Registry ToS](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/terms-of-service)). writ would consume it for server pinning rather than depend on it.

**Identity providers.** Delegation is converging on token exchange. Okta's Cross App Access is its implementation of the IETF ID-JAG draft and underpins MCP's enterprise-managed authorization extension ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/id-jag-cross-app-access)). Entra Agent ID uses on-behalf-of with agent identity blueprints and advises against passthrough ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5766622/example-for-on-behalf-of-flow-in-azure-ai-foundry)). Auth0 pairs Token Vault exchange with CIBA for asynchronous approval ([Auth0](https://auth0.com/blog/auth0-token-vault-secure-token-exchange-for-ai-agents/)). writ's Biscuit tokens complement these flows rather than compete with them. The WIMSE delegation-chain draft notes that RFC 8693's `act` claim "cannot enforce attenuation" beyond one hop and that each hop needs a round trip to the authorization server ([IETF draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-asor-wimse-agent-delegation-chain-03)). That is precisely the intra-task narrowing writ does offline. The integration that makes writ credible is to accept an inbound OIDC, ID-JAG or OBO token at `writ run`, map `sub`, `act` and the agent ID into Biscuit authority facts, and replace today's unverified `--principal`. NIST's NCCoE concept paper sets the same direction: agents as identifiable entities in enterprise identity systems, built on OAuth, OIDC, SCIM and SPIFFE ([NCCoE](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/accelerating-the-adoption-of-software-and-ai-agent-identity-and-authorization-concept-paper.pdf)).

**Policy engines.** Each major engine now has an MCP story. OPA-Envoy is the default ext_authz decision point ([OPA](https://openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/envoy-introduction)). OpenFGA models `tool#can_call` and filters tool lists ([OpenFGA](https://openfga.dev/docs/use-cases/mcp-server-authorization)). Cerbos sells itself as an MCP decision point with FastMCP middleware ([Cerbos](https://www.cerbos.dev/blog/mcp-authorization)). writ should be the enforcement point plus audit and replay layer, able to consume these engines as decision points or entitlement sources and to emit Cedar for AgentCore. OpenFGA `Check`/`ListObjects` maps almost one-to-one onto writ's `lookup:` and `in` conditions, which makes it the natural first real entitlement backend.

**Observability.** OpenTelemetry's MCP semantic conventions name spans `{mcp.method.name} {target}` with `gen_ai.tool.name`, but they were still in Development status as of May 2026 ([Greptime](https://greptime.com/blogs/2026-05-09-opentelemetry-genai-semantic-conventions)). writ should emit those spans with `writ.*` decision attributes (rule, outcome, approval id, taint) and export the JSONL log to Splunk, Datadog or Elastic. writ's chain is ahead of most vendors. TrueFoundry, for example, claims a "tamper-evident" log without describing a mechanism ([TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/enterprise-mcp-governance-control-audit-secure-mcp-server-access)). But a local chain cannot detect truncation, a gap one small project explicitly flags ([mcp-policy-gateway](https://github.com/Spuddy10345/mcp-policy-gateway)). writ already logs denials, which matters because some implementations leave pre-dispatch rejections untraced ([Vendure](https://docs.vendure.io/current/framework/reference/core-plugins/mcp-plugin/mcp-tool-call-log)).

**Approvals.** The market default is asynchronous Slack approval keyed to a tool-call ID, resolved once, with timeout meaning deny ([Trigger.dev](https://trigger.dev/human-in-the-loop-ai-agents); [HumanLayer](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/humanlayer)). writ's pause machinery already has the right shape: a durable pending decision, timeout to deny, and re-checks before forwarding. What it lacks is delivery. `writ sudo` should become one backend among CLI, Slack, webhook (for ServiceNow or Jira) and Auth0 CIBA.

**Deployment and CI.** The established Kubernetes pattern is a decision-point sidecar behind Envoy ext_authz ([OPA](https://openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/envoy-introduction)). ToolHive shows an MCP-specific operator with structured audit ([ToolHive](https://docs.stacklok.com/toolhive/guides-k8s/logging)). The cheapest first step for writ is a per-pod sidecar serving today's Unix socket over a shared volume, followed by an ext_authz mode for shared gateways and a GitHub Action that runs `writ replay` on pull requests that change policy.

**Agent frameworks.** Every major framework has a pre-tool hook. The Claude Agent SDK's PreToolUse can return allow, deny, ask or defer, and a hook's allow does not override deny rules, so writ could only narrow ([Claude Agent SDK hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/hooks); [permissions](https://code.claude.com/docs/fr/agent-sdk/permissions.md)). This adapter matters more than any other because it covers non-MCP built-in tools such as Bash and Write, the exact gap the shell incidents exposed. Sub-agent spawns arrive as tool calls, so the same hook can call `writ fork` automatically. The OpenAI Agents SDK's MCP `require_approval` callable does not receive call arguments ([OpenAI Agents SDK](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/mcp/)), so argument-level decisions there must come from `writ wrap` at the transport layer or from tool guardrails. LangGraph, Google ADK and CrewAI hooks were not verified in this research.

## Writ's edge is the combination, not any single feature

The market has four layers, and writ belongs to none of them. **Classifier guardrails** were almost entirely absorbed into platform suites in 2025. Snyk bought Invariant, Palo Alto bought Protect AI, SentinelOne bought Prompt Security, Check Point bought Lakera and Cato bought Aim. Reported prices vary and conflict across sources ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/securing-ais-ma-feeding-frenzy-is-on/)). Classifiers are probabilistic, and EchoLeak's bypass of Microsoft's classifier shows their ceiling. **MCP gateways** mostly authorize which user may call which tool and leave per-user business rules to the tools. Examples are Pomerium's PPL, Kong's enterprise MCP plugins, Tyk, Lunar MCPX with approval workflows, Cloudflare portals, and agentgateway with CEL plus OPA, OpenFGA and SpiceDB ([Speakeasy](https://www.speakeasy.com/blog/best-mcp-gateways-for-enterprise-2026); [agentgateway](https://agentgateway.dev/blog/2026-06-04-agentgateway-joins-aaif/)). **Agent identity vendors** are well funded and solve who the agent is, not whether a particular call is within the principal's authority. Keycard raised $38M ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/keycard-emerges-from-stealth-mode-with-38-million-in-funding/)) and Arcade raised $60M and bought Smithery ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260615229631/en/Arcade-Raises-$60M-to-Become-the-Secure-Action-Layer-Behind-Every-Production-AI-Agent)); Okta and Auth0 work in the same layer. **General authorization engines** can express entitlement-aware argument rules, but only as integration work. Agent-security platforms are also raising large rounds: Zenity's $125M Series C ([Zenity](https://zenity.io/press-release/zenity-raises-125-million-to-secure-the-era-of-1-billion-ai-agents)) and HiddenLayer's $100M Series B ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/hiddenlayer-raises-100-million-for-ai-runtime-security/)).

Against that field, writ's individual features are not unique, and it should not claim they are. Small open-source proxies already offer hash-chained logs, dry-run and approvals ([agentguard](https://github.com/agentwares/agentguard)), and simulation against recorded traffic ([mcp-gate](https://github.com/rsh1k/mcp-gate)). One even has a policy simulator with shadow mode that diffs a candidate policy against past calls ([HarrisonCN/mcp-gateway](https://github.com/HarrisonCN/mcp-gateway/pull/86)). Biscuit-based agent delegation exists as a PyPI package ([agent-identity-protocol](https://pypi.org/project/agent-identity-protocol/)). Invariant's toxic flow analysis predicts trifecta risk statically ([Invariant](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/toxic-flow-analysis)). Academic reference monitors outperform writ's coarse taint on benchmarks. Progent reportedly cut AgentDojo injection success from 39.9% to 1.0% with argument-level privilege policies ([arXiv 2504.11703](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11703)). CaMeL provides provable security on a subset of tasks by separating control flow from data flow ([arXiv 2503.18813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18813v1)).

What is distinctive is the **combination in one deterministic, MCP-native enforcement layer**. That combination is runtime taint that escalates to a human, Biscuit tokens that sub-agents narrow offline under the same policy and audit, a replay that fails CI when a policy loosens (a monotonicity check, not just a diff), and entitlement lookups as first-class YAML. The sources reviewed name no commercial product enforcing runtime taint or sub-agent attenuation, though Zenity, Noma, Operant and others were not checked at the documentation level. writ also fills gaps the IETF drafts name: the WIMSE draft says Biscuit "does not natively encode" delegation roles or depth limits ([IETF draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-asor-wimse-agent-delegation-chain-03)), and writ adds `max_depth` and per-fork limits on top. The fifth differentiator is neutrality. As classifiers are absorbed into SASE, EDR and AppSec suites, an Apache-2.0 component that plugs into agentgateway, Envoy or Docker is scarcer. The flip side is that writ often competes against a free module bundled into an existing platform contract ([aioutlooks](https://aioutlooks.com/compare-ai-security-solutions/)). The natural partners are identity vendors as principal sources, gateways as the network edge, and OpenFGA, Cerbos or SpiceDB as entitlement stores. That is a coherent position: writ supplies the per-call business-rule authorization and evidence that every other layer leaves out.

## Becoming real means plumbing first, then evidence, then scale

**The compliance clock gives writ a window.** The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) moved the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations for standalone Annex III systems to **2 December 2027** and for product-embedded AI to 2 August 2028 ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-omnibus-20260/)). Those obligations include automatic logging (Art. 12), human oversight (Art. 14) and log retention of at least six months (Arts. 19 and 26(6)) ([Pipelab](https://pipelab.org/learn/eu-ai-act-compliance/)). EU buyers will therefore run procurement through 2027. Sector rules set harder numbers. PCI DSS 10.5.1 requires 12 months of log history with 3 months immediately available ([Daydream](https://learn.daydream.ai/requirements/pci-dss-10-5-1)). HIPAA retention is commonly stated as six years, though sources disagree on the legal basis ([Netizen](https://www.netizen.net/news/post/7735/audit-log-retention-what-pci-dss-nist-hipaa-and-fedramp-expect)). DORA's RTS requires detecting failures of the logging system itself ([SpringLex](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-12/)). SOC 2 auditors expect logs stored separately from the generating system, with after-the-fact integrity proof, typically WORM storage or hashes in a separate store ([ComplyJet](https://www.complyjet.com/blog/logging-stack-soc-2-auditor)). FINRA's 2026 report flags agents "acting without human validation or approval" and exceeding the user's mandate ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/12/16/finra-issues-2026-regulatory-oversight-report/)). writ's chain already exceeds ISO 42001's A.6.2.8, which does not require linked records ([AgenticRail](https://agenticrail-web.pages.dev/blog/iso-42001-a628-event-log/)). Its compliance gaps are external anchoring, export, retention tiers and logging-failure alarms.

**Operationally, writ already clears the latency bar but not the availability bar.** Cedar evaluates in a median of about 4–11 µs ([Cedar paper](https://cdn.amazon.science/96/a8/1b427993481cbdf0ef2c8ca6db85/cedar-a-new-language-for-expressive-fast-safe-and-analyzable-authorization.pdf)), and the ecosystem default for inline authorizers is fail-closed, as in Envoy's `failure_mode_allow: false` ([OPA docs](https://openpolicyagent.org/docs/v0.56.0/envoy-performance)). writ measures **1.6–1.9 ms p99 with fsync and about 0.1–0.4 ms without**, and it fails closed when writd is unreachable ([README](../../README.md)). Its remaining operational gaps are a stateless replicated decision path, durable shared approval and revocation state, multi-tenancy, and keys held in a KMS. On developer experience, mature authorization ecosystems combine four things: schema validation, unit tests, per-rule audit-versus-enforce modes (Kyverno, whose users were confused when "Audit" policies enforced anyway ([kyverno#716](https://github.com/kyverno/policies/discussions/716))), and policies bootstrapped from traffic, as with AWS IAM Access Analyzer's 90-day CloudTrail generation ([AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/access-analyzer-policy-generation.html)). writ's replay is the strongest piece of this toolkit, and the other three are missing.

**Approvals must avoid becoming push-bombing.** CISA documents how context-free approve/deny prompts get reflexively approved and recommends number matching as a stopgap ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/fact-sheet-implement-number-matching-in-mfa-applications-508c.pdf)). Agent-approval guidance from one vendor asks for four things: approvals bound to the exact call and arguments, typed confirmation of high-risk values, monitoring for rubber-stamping, and rate limits, because a manipulated agent can flood approvers ([AgentFort](https://agentfort.net/agent-security/human-approval)). No study measuring how often humans rubber-stamp agent approvals was found, so the risk reduction approvals deliver remains unquantified. On adoption, the precedents are consistent. OPA and Falco grew under neutral foundation governance with open-core commercial layers ([CNCF](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2024/02/29/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-falco-graduation/)). HashiCorp's 2023 BSL switch spawned OpenTofu and OpenBao ([WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/open-source-licence-change-chronicle/)). writ should keep its core under Apache-2.0 and monetize a control plane, compliance packs and support.

Second, the outside security review of the token design, which matters doubly because gateway authentication bugs are a recurring attack class.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what writ is for. It is not a general agent firewall, a role already filled by platforms that classify text. It is the **authorization and evidence layer for business-semantic tool calls**: the place where "this refund belongs to this customer, this agent read a ticket first, a human approved this exact call, and the new policy is no looser than the old" becomes enforceable and provable. That niche sits between every layer the market has funded. Identity vendors stop at who the agent is, gateways at which tool, classifiers at which text. Each of them exposes a hook writ can plug into, which makes integration, not head-on competition, the realistic route.

The biggest strategic risk is scope inflation rather than missing features. Most headline incidents, such as EchoLeak, postmark-mcp, mcp-remote and the shell wipes, are ones a tool-call proxy cannot stop, and buyers who discover that on their own will discount the rest. writ earns credibility by publishing what it does not cover, shipping the hook adapter that pulls shell and file tools under policy, and pairing itself explicitly with sandboxes and scoped credentials. The December 2027 EU deadline and the 84% who doubt they could pass an audit point to the second lever. writ's hash chain and replay become a compliance product only once the log leaves the machine, anchored and exported, and principals are verified rather than asserted. Those two changes turn writ from a safety tool for developers into evidence an organization can buy.
